# Fan A maintenance notes (given to the explainer as context)

Before touching the fan: switch it off at its breaker, wait for the rotor to stop, and keep the backup fan running.

## imbalance
Signs: a strong tone at the rotation rate, amplitude wobble, a slap once per revolution.
Likely causes: debris, tape or ice on a blade, a cracked or bent blade, a loose blade clip.
Checks: stop the fan and inspect the blades for debris; spin by hand and look for wobble; check blade fasteners.
Fix: remove debris or tape from the blades; retighten blade clips; replace a cracked or bent blade (spare blade kit, bay 3).
Tools: flashlight, 3 mm hex key, isopropyl wipes.

## bearing
Signs: a steady high-pitched whine at 1-6 kHz that slowly rises in pitch.
Likely causes: bearing wear or dry lubrication.
Checks: listen at the motor end cap; feel for heat at the bearing; spin by hand and feel for roughness.
Fix: relubricate the bearing (2 drops of light machine oil at the end-cap port); if it still whines or feels rough, replace the motor (spare motor, bay 3).
Tools: machine oil, 3 mm hex key, flashlight.

## loose mount
Signs: rattle, broadband bursts, extra low-frequency energy.
Likely causes: loose mounting screws, worn isolation grommets, the housing touching a panel.
Checks: check mount screws for torque; inspect isolation grommets; look for contact with nearby panels.
Fix: retighten the 4 mount screws; replace cracked grommets; move cables or panels that touch the housing.
Tools: Phillips screwdriver, spare grommets.

## air leak
Signs: a steady broadband hiss, strongest above 3 kHz.
Likely causes: a loose duct clamp, a split duct seam, or a gap at the filter housing.
Checks: run a hand or a tissue along the duct joints to feel for escaping air; check the duct clamps; check the filter housing seal.
Fix: retighten the clamp; seal a split seam with aluminium duct tape; reseat the filter housing. A leak in the pressure hull itself is an emergency: follow the habitat leak procedure instead.
Tools: aluminium duct tape, flat screwdriver, tissue.

## friction
Signs: broadband scrape at 2-6 kHz, slight speed drop.
Likely causes: a blade rubbing the shroud, a cable or foreign object touching the rotor, a dry bearing.
Checks: look for rub marks on the shroud; clear cables and objects from the rotor; listen at the bearing.
Fix: clear the object; recentre the shroud by loosening its 4 screws, centring and retightening.
Tools: Phillips screwdriver, flashlight.

## unknown anomaly
Signs: the sound changed in a way that doesn't match a known fault.
New high-pitched tones (1-6 kHz) often mean bearing wear. Bursts of broadband noise often mean something is loose.
Checks: compare with the last maintenance log; inspect the fan and its mount; if it persists, switch to the backup fan.
Fix: switch to the backup fan, then inspect blades, mount, bearing and ducts in that order.
